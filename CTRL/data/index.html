ctrl/chapter-01

Start with the data.

Load the observations, understand what they represent, and turn a market question into a reproducible experiment.

core question

What could you actually know when a decision was made?

In this chapter.

01Understand the observations

Begin with the meaning of each field. A price quote, an order-book snapshot and a completed trade describe different things; they should not be treated as interchangeable.

  • Record the product, timestamp, price and volume conventions.
  • Separate prices, trades and any additional observations.
  • Identify the edition and round before combining files.
02Load, inspect & organise

Keep the original files and build a consistent research view alongside them. A clean dataset should still make it possible to trace an observation back to its source.

  • Check separators, types, missing values and repeated timestamps.
  • Inspect spreads, depth and trades by product and time.
  • Document transformations and the availability time of each feature.
03Ask one question at a time

Write down the pattern you expect before searching for the best parameters. Explore on one period and reserve a later period for a fair comparison.

  • Compare each idea with a simple baseline.
  • Separate feature construction from future outcomes.
  • Keep a record of trials, assumptions and unsuccessful ideas.
