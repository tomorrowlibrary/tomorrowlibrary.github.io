study 001

One order book, three editions

Every edition opens with a product anchored at 10,000. Is it a new market each time?

  • 30,000 of 30,000round 1 snapshots of PEARLS (P1), AMETHYSTS (P2) and RAINFOREST_RESIN (P3) are identical: every price, volume and timestamp.
  • 16.6%of those snapshots had a quote on the wrong side of 10,000, about 1,655 a day. Free edge for a bot that takes it.
  • ±8Prosperity 4 changed it. EMERALDS sat at 9,992 / 10,008 in 98% of snapshots, and no snapshot offered a mispriced quote.

Data and method

The round 1 price files of Prosperity 1 to 3 and the Prosperity 4 tutorial. After filtering each file to its anchored product, all twelve book columns plus day and timestamp were compared row by row. The trade files are not identical, so only the book repeated.

Why it matters

On the first day of a round, test the new data for exact matches against the archive. A matching book means known quoting patterns and a known edge. Prosperity 4 shows the organisers can and do change the generator.

Caveats

These are the published historical files. Rounds were scored on fresh simulations, which need not repeat the history exactly.

study 002

Basket premiums revert, and the basket does the moving

How large is the basket premium, how fast does it come back, and which side moves when it does?

  • 376 and 380average premiums for the Prosperity 1 and 2 baskets; the Prosperity 3 baskets carried 49 and 30.
  • 197 to 762iterations: the half-life of a deviation, from an AR(1) fit within each day. Fast enough to trade many times a day.
  • the basket movesAfter a deviation the basket closes 3.4% of the gap every 10 iterations in P2. The ingredients close none of it: in every edition they drift up to 0.5% the other way.

Data and method

Mid prices for each basket and its ingredients over the three days of the round that introduced it. Spread = basket − Σ weight × ingredient. The share of the gap each leg closes is the slope of its 10-iteration price change on the demeaned spread; a negative share means that leg drifted away.

Why it matters

Trade the basket toward the synthetic, not the reverse. Hedging with the ingredients cuts variance but adds spread costs without adding expected value, which matches Frankfurt Hedgehogs' account.

Caveats

Mids are not tradable prices, so these numbers ignore the cost of crossing the spread. The premium moves between days: Prosperity 1's dropped by about 75 on day 3.

study 003

The trader who knew the extremes

When trader names were revealed, did anyone buy the day's low and sell the day's high?

  • 6 of 6of Rhianna's ROSES trades in Prosperity 2 sat at an extreme: buys on average 0.3% up from the day's low, sells 99.8% of the way to the high.
  • 13 of 13of Olivia's SQUID_INK and CROISSANTS trades in Prosperity 3 did the same. Her KELP trades came close without touching the extremes.
  • 0 of 7Prosperity 4 round 4 IDs showed the pattern. The closest, Mark 01 on VEV_5200, had 9% of trades at an extreme.

Data and method

Named trades from Prosperity 2 round 5 (rounds 1, 3 and 4 re-released), Prosperity 3 round 5 and Prosperity 4 round 4. Each trade is placed between the day's lowest and highest mid for its product. An extreme is a buy in the bottom 5% or a sell in the top 5%.

Why it matters

The pattern survived three editions and paid well. Without names, Frankfurt Hedgehogs caught it by flagging trades of a recurring size at a new running daily extreme.

Caveats

The day's range is only known at the close, so this is hindsight. A live detector must use the running minimum and maximum, and will see false alarms before the real extreme arrives.
