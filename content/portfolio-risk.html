Define the unit of exposure

A signal, an order and a portfolio weight are different objects. Specify whether a number is a quantity, currency notional, fraction of equity, volatility target or risk contribution. Keep cash and leverage explicit.

A basic long-only allocation has non-negative weights summing to at most one; the remainder is cash. The workbench includes equal weights, inverse-volatility weights and per-asset caps. Its cap helper leaves excess allocation in cash rather than silently redistributing it.

Risk depends on joint behaviour

Volatility weighting is a useful baseline, but it ignores cross-asset dependence. Portfolio variance is wᵀΣw. Estimating and inverting a noisy covariance matrix can create unstable allocations, so compare optimisation with simple baselines and inspect sensitivity to estimation choices.

Inspect paths and tails

Maximum drawdown measures the largest decline from a previous equity peak over the observed path. It is sensitive to the sample and marking frequency. Include the initial capital in the path so a loss at the first evaluation point is counted.

Historical VaR is a quantile of a loss sample. Expected shortfall averages losses in the selected tail. The workbench uses loss = −return: positive values represent losses and negative values represent gains. Neither estimate is a guarantee about the next extreme event.
